What to build first

Build an Uber-like app in three stages, not all at once

Most of the features people associate with big ride-hailing apps came after years of real trips. This is the order we usually recommend.

  1. Stage 1

    Launch

    Prove the core loop in one city.

    • Rider booking and fare estimate
    • Driver app and ride requests
    • Real-time matching
    • Live tracking
    • Payments
    • Ratings
    • Admin basics
  2. Stage 2

    Grow

    Add what real trips ask for.

    • Scheduled rides
    • Promo codes
    • More vehicle types
    • Driver earnings reports
    • In-app support
  3. Stage 3

    Scale

    Run it as a bigger business.

    • Dynamic pricing rules
    • More cities and areas
    • Deeper analytics
    • Corporate accounts
    • Extra payment methods

Clone or original?

What to learn from Uber, and what not to copy

An Uber-like app is inspired by a proven model. It should never look like, or pretend to be, another company’s product. We build original apps with no other brand’s name, logo or code in them.

Learn from the model

  • Request a ride with a tap
  • Upfront fare estimate
  • Live tracking and arrival time
  • Two-way ratings
  • Cashless payment as an option

Make it yours

  • Your own name, logo and identity
  • Your pricing, commission and payout model
  • Local payment methods and languages
  • Local licensing, insurance and driver rules
  • A launch plan for your city

Under the hood

The real-time engine behind a ride-hailing app

Matching a rider to a driver in seconds depends on keeping fresh positions flowing and searchable. Weak GPS, dropped connections and battery use are the problems we design around.

  1. Step 1

    Locations stream in

    Driver apps send their position to the backend every few seconds while online.

  2. Step 2

    The latest position is kept

    The backend holds each driver's current position and status, ready to search.

  3. Step 3

    A request finds a driver

    The matching service looks for nearby available drivers and offers the ride.

  4. Step 4

    Both apps stay in sync

    Push and live connections update rider and driver as the trip moves on.

Launching the marketplace

Getting both sides of the marketplace on board

  • Start in one city

    A focused launch area keeps drivers and riders close enough for fast matches.

  • Recruit drivers first

    Riders leave quickly if nobody is nearby, so have drivers online before you open bookings.

  • Make onboarding simple

    Upload documents in the app, review them in the admin, and approve drivers without delay.
