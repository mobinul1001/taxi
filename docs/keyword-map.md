## Overlaps to watch (cannibalization risks)

1. **"Taxi app development" appears in the title or H1 of about seven pages** (home, development, services, solutions, cost, custom, Uber-like). Only /taxi-app-development/ should lead with the bare phrase. The others should lead with their own modifier (services, solutions, cost, custom). Services and solutions both start with "Taxi App Development" today.
2. **/taxi-app-development-cost/ vs /pricing/.** Both answer "how much". Keep cost as the explanation of what drives budget and pricing as the package page, and make each link to the other with different wording. Do not give both the same title pattern.
3. **/taxi-app-development-services/ vs /taxi-app-solutions/ vs /taxi-app-development/.** Three broad pages with similar lead phrases. Distinct jobs: overview, deliverables, fit by business type. Keep each page's first screen about its own job.
4. **/uber-clone-app-development/ URL vs "Uber-like" copy.** The slug says "clone" while title, H1 and links say "Uber-like". Planned fix: rename to /uber-like-taxi-app-development/ with a 301 from the old URL. Confirm "uber clone" is a phrase you want to keep as a secondary term before dropping it from the URL.
5. **Blog vs hub pages.** Blog posts should answer a narrow question and link up to a hub. They should not repeat a hub's target phrase in their title.

## What needs real data before any title change

- Search volume and difficulty for each main target (keyword tool).
- Current impressions, clicks and average position per URL (Search Console, once the site is verified and has traffic).
- Which pages Google treats as duplicates of each other, if any (Search Console Pages report).

## Decisions for the owner

1. Keep or drop "uber clone" as a secondary term.
2. Approve rewriting services and solutions titles so they do not both lead with "Taxi App Development".
3. Provide Search Console access or exports so the table above can be checked against real queries.
